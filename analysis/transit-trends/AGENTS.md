# Transit over time (`analysis/transit-trends/`)

Agent guide. Start with the root [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md). What the page shows, its method in plain words, limits and sources for readers are in [`README.md`](README.md); keep it current when the page changes. This file is for changing the code: where every number comes from, how it is computed, and what went wrong before.

`docs/transit-trends.html` (somerwalks.org/transit-trends.html, "Riding transit in Somerville"): weekday riding on Somerville's buses, the T stations its people use, and Bluebikes, every fall since 2016; who's working; and, for the latest fall, how full and how fast each bus route is. All from public data, labeled as PTAC's analysis.

## Build

`npm run trends` runs `build.py` (Python standard library only). It reads the sources below, writes `output/trends.html` from `trends_template.html` (the data replaces `/*DATA*/null`, so the page is one self-contained file) and copies it to `docs/transit-trends.html`: never edit the copy.

Everything downloaded is cached in `../../.cache/` (about 1.5 GB). `cached()` downloads a file only when it isn't there: **a cached file is never refreshed; delete it to get a newer version.** The build prints each season's totals (bus riders and stops, rail per station, gate entries, Bluebikes, survey, jobs, profiles, speeds): compare them with the previous run before committing.

## A new fall (the MBTA adds one each year, usually by the next summer)

1. **Bus ridership:** delete `.cache/mbta-bus-ridership.zip`; the build then reads the new season's CSV (and its column names, which change: see "Pitfalls").
2. **Stop locations:** automatic (the last archived feed of each fall, from `archived_feeds.txt`).
3. **T boardings:** add the new rail file to `RAIL_ITEMS` (kind `csv` for a downloadable CSV, `service` for a feature service with `parent_station`, `old` for the 2017–19 service keyed by `stop_name`). Check its rows per route, direction and hour, and its `number_service_days`.
4. **Fare-gate entries:** delete `.cache/mbta-gated-entries.zip` (the historical zip gains a file a year).
5. **Bus speeds:** add the new year's MBTA Bus Arrival Departure Times item to `BUS_TIMES`; without it the speed section is left out and the build warns.
6. **Bluebikes, the survey, LODES:** automatic. Survey years from the last two are retried each build until published; LODES lags about two years.
7. Rebuild, compare the printed totals, read the page, and audit (see "How to check it").

## How each number is computed

- **Bus riders in Somerville (`bus()`):** for each fall, sum the average boardings of every weekday trip at every stop inside Somerville, the stop placed with that fall's feed. From Fall 2025 the file splits `Wkdy` (Monday–Thursday) and `Fri`: weekday = (4 × Wkdy + Fri) / 5. A stop id missing from the fall's last feed takes its location from the nearest other fall's feed. Saturday and Sunday the same way.
- **Routes (`bus()`):** riders by route at Somerville stops (`747` shown as CT2, `194` as 89/93; routes under `MIN_ROUTE` = 50 riders every fall dropped). Trips: distinct weekday trips (variant, direction, start time) with at least one Somerville stop, Mon–Thu (`Wkdy`) from 2025.
- **T stations (`rail()`):** weekday `average_ons` summed over a station's rows; for the 2025 CSV, `total_ons` summed and divided by the station's most `number_service_days` (rows have different day counts). Stations: the seven in Somerville plus Porter and Sullivan Square (`STATIONS`).
- **Fare-gate entries (`gates()`):** average weekday entries, Sep–Nov, federal holidays out, days with no entries at a station (closures, shutdowns) left out. Shown only as hatched bars for the falls without boardings (2016, 2020–22) and as a dashed "T fare gates" line, the same measure every fall; never added into a boardings number.
- **Headline, tiles:** latest fall against Fall 2019. The headline's "about as many / more / fewer residents have jobs" comes from `beyondMargin()`: two survey estimates differ only when the gap is bigger than √(m0² + m1²) (90% margins).
- **Bluebikes (`bluebikes()`):** trips that start or end at a Somerville dock, each trip once, split into within (both ends), leaving, arriving; averaged over Sep–Nov weekdays (federal holidays out, Veterans Day as observed) and weekend days. Each station is placed at its dock from classic-bike trips (from 2024 e-bike trips carry the bike's GPS position). Docks are counted as places (`places()`: ids within 40 m are one dock; "Temp Winter Station" ids left out). E-bike share from `rideable_type` (marked only from 2024).
- **Who's working (`acs()`, `lodes()`):** way to work from B08006 (shares of its total, "residents who worked"); "residents with a job" is B23025 employed; share 65+ from B01001. LODES summed over the blocks the crosswalk puts in "Somerville city".
- **When people ride (`bus()`):** weekday riders at Somerville stops by the hour the trip started (hours before 3 am folded to 24+; Fall 2025 has some start times as fractions of a day: `start_hour()`). The note computes the 5–9 am and 9 am–3 pm shares of 2019 in the page.
- **Map:** each stop's weekday riders in two falls; stop ids within `SAME_STOP` (12 m) across falls are one place (busiest first). Gone and new stops are counted in the note.
- **How full (`trip_loads()`, `profiles()`):** each weekday departure's load (riders on board leaving each stop) in Fall 2019 and the latest fall, Mon–Thu in 2025, departures counted on fewer than 3 days dropped. Stops are placed by distance along the latest feed's path for that direction (the longest path run on at least 5% as many trips as the most-run one). Left out: stops more than 150 m off the path, stops past its ends, and, for the averages, stops served by under 30% of the departures. Thin lines: each departure (the page draws every one); thick: the average per stop. "Over 36": departures whose highest load passes 36 seats.
- **How fast (`bus_runs()`, in `profiles()`):** every weekday run Sep–Nov of the latest fall's year (holidays out); for consecutive timing stops on a run, distance along the same path divided by the actual time between them, in mph (kept between 1 and 45). Runs are grouped by scheduled departure (median per stretch). Per direction one chain of stretches that don't overlap (variants time different stops; the most-run stretches win); stretches with under 20 runs and departures with under 5 runs dropped. Overall speed: total distance of the stretches over the sum of distance / median speed.

- **New neighbors near the T (`homes()`):** reads `restricted_housing.csv` (in this folder; one row per Schedule W entry: label, units, status, first permit, occupied, permit numbers, latitude, longitude), checked by hand from the City's permit applications (`nneb-s3f7`: the building permit's description gives the units, the certificate of occupancy the date); people = homes × the latest ACS B25010 renter household size (`renter_household()`, cached `trends-acs-b25010-<year>.json`). Draws the T lines and the profile routes' bus paths (from `route_paths()`, every other point) and ½-mile circles around `HOME_STATIONS`. The section starts at `HOMES_FROM` (2025): built buildings occupied before it are left off the map and the tiles and named in the note; the table lists every address. **To update:** download the current Schedule W PDF from the Parking Department page, add new addresses, look each up in the permit applications (new-construction building permits and certificates of occupancy at those numbers), update units, status and dates; coordinates from the permit rows. How it was done the first time: [`research/parking-restricted-housing.md`](../../research/parking-restricted-housing.md).

## Pitfalls (found in the Nov 2026 audit and while building; keep the fixes)

- **Simplified outlines misclassify the edge.** The map's outline (about 20 m simplified) put a Cambridge Bluebikes dock (Inman Square at Springfield St, the busiest "Somerville" dock in 2019–20) and a bus stop (Elm St at St James Ave) on the wrong side. Every inside test uses `somerville_exact()`. Bluebikes ids from 2023 carry a city prefix (S = Somerville): every dock inside should start with S.
- **The fall's last feed can miss stops** that had riders earlier that fall (detours, closures): 14 Somerville stops and 49 riders in 2019. Fall back to the nearest fall's feed.
- **Columns and codes change by year.** Load: `load_` to 2023, `load` 2024, `Load` 2025; samples: `sample_size`, `sample_count`, `# of Trip Samples ` (trailing space); day types `weekday` vs `Wkdy`/`Fri`; start times `7:05:00` or a fraction of a day; route ids `CT2` in arrival times vs `747` in schedules; `Inbound` = direction 1. Map them explicitly (`COLS`) and expect a new spelling each year.
- **A blank table isn't a missing year.** The Census lists Somerville in B08301 for 2022 and 2023 with every value blank; B08006 has the same counts. Treat an empty `_001E` as missing and look for a sibling table. 2020 has no 1-year tables at all; 5-year tables mix years and aren't a substitute.
- **Say "more" only beyond the margin.** "More residents have jobs" was inside the survey's margin, and payroll counts went the other way; transit commuters' drop was inside it too. Use `beyondMargin()` and say "about as many".
- **One name, one number.** "Residents with a job" once meant two different counts on the same page. Employed (B23025) is "with a job"; the commute total is "who worked".
- **Boundary lists drift.** A tract range for Somerville took in a Medford block (about 240 jobs). Use the LODES crosswalk.
- **Check jumps before explaining them.** Jobs in Somerville jump in 2018: about 5,800 "management of companies" jobs first counted at one block (tract 350105). The note states that and no more (no employer named without a source).
- **"No data" may only mean one dataset.** The MBTA publishes no station boardings for 2016 and 2020–22, but its gate entries cover them; say which.
- **Two measures of the same thing can drift.** Boardings ran 0–5% above gate entries in 2017–19 and up to 57% above at Assembly by 2025 (4,891 vs 3,122). The stations note warns; don't headline Assembly's rise.
- **Different day counts can't be averaged by adding averages.** The 2025 rail CSV's Green Line branches serve the Medford stations on different numbers of days: add totals, divide by days.
- **One special trip can look like the average.** School trips on the 95 and 101 (one departure on Winthrop St) doubled the peak of the per-stop average. Drop stops served by under 30% of departures.
- **Name-only records duplicate places.** A few Fall 2025 Bluebikes trips give the station name with a blank id; keyed by name they became extra docks (39 → 43). Map names to ids (`n2id`).
- **Count ids as places.** Renumbered docks (2018) and temporary winter docks (2022–24) inflated dock counts; `places()` merges them.
- **Count the same end.** Bus and T boardings count riders getting on in Somerville; Bluebikes counts trips with either end in Somerville. Don't stack them or add them; Bluebikes stays on its own chart and on the 2019 comparison.
- **Overlapping stretches.** Route variants time different stops, so speed stretches A–C and A–B, B–C overlap; keep one chain.
- **Sources for every sentence.** Removed or fixed in the audit: "far from the line" for Lechmere (it is about 220 m from Somerville; the reason given is the industrial land next to it), "counts come from samples" for the T (unsupported), the GLX "no fare gates" (now sourced to mbta.com), the 86 and 109 changes (sourced). Never state ride-hail is in the survey's "other" (unconfirmed).

## How to check it

- **Recompute independently.** The Nov 2026 audit had two agents recompute every bus, rail and Bluebikes number from the raw files without reading `build.py` first; it confirmed the arithmetic and found the outline, missing-stop and Bluebikes errors above. Do the same after a big change, and diff against the printed totals.
- **Read the page as a reader.** Every sentence: a source in `research/sources.md` or the data itself? Every comparison of survey years within its margin? Every "no data" saying which data?
- **Browser:** desktop and 390 px; no sideways scroll, no console errors, hover every chart type; the route sections draw 56 charts (about 50 ms).

## Choices to keep

- **Stations:** the seven inside Somerville plus Porter and Sullivan Square (`STATIONS`). Lechmere is left out on purpose (close to the line, about 220 m, but next to industrial land rather than homes, and few in Somerville use it). Change the list only with a reason on the page.
- **Somerville stops per season,** with that season's stop locations, never today's list (the 86 left Somerville in Dec 2024, the 109 came in). The 109 has no 2019 line anywhere; the 86 isn't profiled.
- **Missing T years** are hatched fare-gate bars labeled "fare gates", never zero and never an empty height.
- **Map stop merge:** `SAME_STOP` 12 m. Tried 25 and 40 m: the gone stops stay, so they are real changes. The map note names the 86 only when the falls picked span Dec 2024.
- **Route lists** (Every route, the load profiles, the speeds, the tables) are in route-number order, CT2 last.
- **Page order:** headline and tiles; the decade of boardings; Bluebikes; back to 2019; why (how residents get to work, people with jobs, when people ride the bus); the stations; where riders went; every route; how full and how fast the buses are (one section: per direction, the load chart then the speed chart below it, same horizontal scale; the load chart is drawn `bare`, without station labels, when a speed chart follows); new neighbors near the T; "All the numbers" and "How it's counted" folded at the end. Notes one line; chart headings one line at 360 px.
- **Weight:** the page is about 310 KB; the departures in the load profiles (about 140 KB) and the speeds (about 45 KB) are most of it.

## Charts and colors

Colors were checked with the dataviz palette validator on the navy surface (`#10243A`): the stacked columns go bus `#4F98DE`, Red `#DA291C`, Green `#1B9B6A`, Orange `#D57200` (red and orange never touch; fare-gate years in the same colors, lighter and hatched); the index chart uses bus blue, the T `#C08A14` (dashed for fare gates), Bluebikes `#2FA37A` and every MBTA bus as a gray reference `#8A9BB0`; Bluebikes stacks within `#2FA37A`, leaving `#67C29A`, arriving `#A6DDC2`; "Who's working" uses worked from home `#C8609C`, transit amber, drove alone gray, walked cyan `#45B7C9` (used nowhere else), biked the Bluebikes green (bikes keep one color) and carpooled dashed gray (a car trip, like drove alone; `lineChart` draws `dash: true` series dashed), then people with jobs in an ice ramp that no other chart uses (residents working `#DCE6F2`, residents' jobs `#A9C4E4`, jobs in Somerville `#7690B0`); the map is diverging orange `#E36F28` (fewer riders) to purple `#9575E3` (more) through gray, new stops a purple ring, gone stops an orange ring, T stations white squares, so on the map orange only ever means fewer and bus blue isn't reused for "more". The route list and cards use the same meaning in text tints (`#F2A374` fewer, `#B9A3F0` more); trips are gray. Load profiles: 2019 gray, latest fall bus blue (thin per departure, thick average). Speeds: amber-gold `#E8B84A`, used nowhere else. Every chart has a legend, selective direct labels, a hover tooltip, an `aria-label`, and its numbers in the table where they fit. Axis scales (`ticks()`) always reach past the largest value. Map station labels are placed biggest first where they overlap nothing (short names on phones); route-chart station labels rank Davis, Harvard, Sullivan, Lechmere, Central, Kendall first.
