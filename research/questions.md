# Snow Guide: Questions for City, MBTA and State Officials

Copy of the PTAC Claude Doc as of October 19, 2026. The live doc is the working version: https://claude.ai/artifact/2oc3c9e1vG2C7EkckRYyr2

Send questions 2 and 6 first: their answers decide whether the guide's statements about legal duties and fines are correct.

These 27 questions and 19 proposals come from building the Somerville PTAC snow guide for walkers and transit riders. Each notes the card it affects. On each question, sources disagree or are silent.

## City: Law Department or Inspectional Services

Ordinance wording and enforcement.

1. **Updating the City's snow page to match the 2025 amendment.** The amended Section 12-8 adds a curb ramp duty for commercial properties and confirms the 36-inch minimum. Will the [City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow) and Ordinance 2025-25 be published together, so residents can read the current rule? The page also says the higher fine starts at "more than four units", but Sec. 1-11 sets it at four or more. *(Cards: 3 feet wide, The ramp too, Run a business)*
   - Current text: Somerville Code of Ordinances, Ch. 12, Sec. 12-8(a) and (a)(1), Ord. No. 2025-25, effective 12/11/2025
2. **Who gets the ticket.** Section 12-8 applies to "a person, including an owner, occupant, tenant, or agent, in charge of land or a building." In practice, are citations issued to tenants and commercial occupants, or only to the owner of record? *(Cards: Run a business, Renting)*
   - Current text: Sec. 12-8(a); the City's pages name owners only ([snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow), [sidewalk clearing pilot](https://www.somervillema.gov/departments/programs/sidewalk-snow-clearing-pilot))
3. **When the City steps in.** Section 12-8(c) lets the City clear and place a lien but sets no timing. Does clean-and-lien start 24 hours after snow stops, or 24 daylight hours after the emergency ends? *(Card: Fines)*
   - Both appear on the [City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow), under Shoveling Rules and under Plowing
4. **Extensions.** Section 12-8 allows the deadline to be "extended by the city in its discretion." How are extensions announced, and does being away ever count? *(Card: Going away)*
   - Current text: Sec. 12-8(a); Brookline and Cambridge say absence is no excuse ([Brookline winter guide](https://www.brooklinema.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53279/2024-Winter-Guide), [Cambridge snow center](https://cambridgema.gov/SNOW))
5. **From ticket to cleared sidewalk.** A ticket does not clear the ice. Section 12-8(c) lets the City clear a neglected sidewalk and place a lien, but ISD writes the tickets and DPW does the clearing. After a ticket, how does ISD hand the address to DPW? Does anyone re-inspect, and how long does a ticketed sidewalk typically stay uncleared? *(Card: Fines)*
   - Enforcement for Sec. 12-8 is split among DPW, Inspectional Services and Traffic and Parking (Code of Ordinances, Sec. 1-11(b)); City clearing and lien (Sec. 12-8(c)); at least 200 fines after one February 2025 storm ([CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/somerville-citations-for-icy-sidewalks))

## City: Public Works

6. **Corner curb ramps.** Section 12-8(a)(1) now requires commercial properties to clear an abutting curb ramp through to the crosswalk, but says nothing of homes. At a residential corner, who clears the ramp: the owner or DPW? *(Cards: The ramp too, Corner lots, Who clears what)*
   - DPW clears "curb cuts and intersection pedestrian landings"; owners clear "accessible ramps that abut their property" ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
   - Owners clear curb cuts on the abutting sidewalk ([Mass.gov: snow removal and accessibility](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/snow-removal-and-accessibility-in-massachusetts))
7. **Crosswalk openings.** Are owners expected to cut through the plow bank to the crosswalk?
   - Required in [Brookline](https://www.brooklinema.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53279/2024-Winter-Guide) and [Cambridge](https://cambridgema.gov/SNOW); corners and ramps named as the worst trouble spots in WalkBoston, *Keep It Clear* ([summary](https://dev.walkboston.org/?p=1654))
8. **Plow-pushed snow.** When a plow re-buries a cleared sidewalk, does the 6-hour clock restart, and is there a grace period? *(Card: Plow buried it again)*
   - Plows pushing snow onto sidewalks "is often unavoidable" ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
9. **Bus stops.** Do the City's 281 stops include stops on MBTA-cleared routes 109, 77 and 104? Does "cleared" include a path to the bus door? *(Card: Your bus stop)*
   - 281 stops ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow)); MBTA-cleared routes ([MBTA: bus service during winter storms](https://www.mbta.com/winter-weather/bus-service-during-winter-storms))
10. **Ice melt.** Does the City have a position on rock salt, sand or alternatives, and will it provide salt barrels? *(Card: Salt or sand)*
    - Cambridge advises against rock salt and sand and provides salt barrels ([Cambridge snow center](https://cambridgema.gov/SNOW)); temperature limits from the [Minnesota Pollution Control Agency](https://stormwater.pca.state.mn.us/How_salt_works_and_overview_of_deicing_chemicals); concrete and environmental effects from the [WJE deicer primer](https://chinesedrywall.wje.com/assets/pdfs/articles/WJE-ThoughtLeadership-PRIMER-Deicer.pdf)
11. **Trash day.** Where should bins go when there's a snowbank?
    - Bins in front of snowbanks, not behind ([Boston winter guide 2025–26](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2025/12/English_0.pdf), [Brookline winter guide](https://www.brooklinema.gov/DocumentCenter/View/53279/2024-Winter-Guide)); no Somerville rule found
12. **Community Path.** Which segments close in storms, who clears the Green Line Extension sections, and where are closures posted? *(Card: Community Path)*
    - [City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow) (Community Path section)
13. **Winter 2026/27 details.** Are the 13 City lots, the tow company, the fees and the Tufts garage arrangement unchanged? *(Parking cards)*
    - [City off-street parking lots](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow/off-street-parking); [City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow) (towing and Tufts)
14. **Sidewalk-clearing pilot.** Is the Broadway and School Street pilot continuing or expanding?
    - [City: Winter Sidewalk Snow Clearing Pilot](https://www.somervillema.gov/departments/programs/sidewalk-snow-clearing-pilot)

## City: data for the "Why it matters" card

18. **Enforcement and complaints.** How many unshoveled-sidewalk complaints reached 311 last winter, and how many citations were issued?
    - Over 400 complaints in two days and at least 200 fines after one February 2025 storm ([CBS Boston, Feb 19 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/somerville-citations-for-icy-sidewalks))
19. **The heart attack claim.** Can Somerville Fire confirm it sees more cardiac calls during heavy-snow shoveling? *(Card: Heart attack snow)*
    - Current card cites the American Heart Association ([AHA Eastern States](https://easternstates.heart.org/?p=13099)); heart attacks from overexertion listed among storm deaths ([Mass.gov winter storm safety](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/winter-storm-safety-tips))
20. **Local injury data.** Do Cambridge Health Alliance or the Health Department track falls on ice?
    - Current card cites a statewide winter spike in hip and forearm fractures, from WalkBoston, *Keep It Clear* (c. 2007, citing studies from 2000 and 2003; [summary](https://dev.walkboston.org/?p=1654))

## Proposals: what the City could publish or start

These go beyond clarifying the rules. Each one asks the City to publish or start something so residents can plan around a storm.

28. **Bus stop clearing order.** The City clears 281 stops "divided into 4 Routes" but does not publish the routes or their order. Publish which stops are cleared first and why (ridership, hospitals, senior housing, schools), so riders know what to expect at their stop. *(Card: Your bus stop)*
    - "281 stops, divided into 4 Routes" ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
29. **One list of every stop and who clears it.** Publish a stop-by-stop list or map showing whether the City or the MBTA clears each stop, using MBTA stop IDs, and update it after the bus network redesign. *(Cards: Your bus stop, Who clears what)*
    - City count ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow)); MBTA's own route list ([MBTA: bus service during winter storms](https://www.mbta.com/winter-weather/bus-service-during-winter-storms))
30. **Clearing stages with expected times.** The City already publishes a priority order: streets first, then municipal lots, then bus stops, ramps and bridges, and on down to State property. Turn it into stages residents can follow, each with a target time after the snow stops (for example, "streets done, bus stops and ramps start"), and announce when each stage begins and ends. *(Cards: Who clears what, Your bus stop)*
    - Eight-step priority order with no timing ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
31. **Results from the sidewalk-clearing pilot.** Publish where City crews cleared private sidewalks, how fast, at what cost, and how complaints compared with streets outside the pilot, then use the results to decide whether to expand it. Builds on question 14.
    - [City: Winter Sidewalk Snow Clearing Pilot](https://www.somervillema.gov/departments/programs/sidewalk-snow-clearing-pilot); "In designated areas, City crews may also clear sidewalks in front of private properties," starting 24 hours after the snow stops ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
32. **A live status page.** One page or map showing what is open and closed right now: Community Path segments, City lots, the snow emergency, cleared bus stops, and which clearing stage the City is in. Today residents piece this together from alerts and several pages. *(Cards: Community Path, City lots, Snow emergency)*
    - 311 requests are "tracked in real-time" internally, but no clearing status is published ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
33. **Data partnerships for "Why it matters."** Seek data-sharing agreements with public and private partners to measure what uncleared sidewalks cost: emergency-room visits for falls (Cambridge Health Alliance and other hospitals), EMS calls (Somerville Fire and the ambulance provider), ridership and The RIDE cancellations on storm days (MBTA), and injury claims from large local employers. Publish the results each spring. Builds on questions 18 to 20. *(Card: Why it matters)*
    - The card now relies on a statewide study from the 2000s (WalkBoston, *Keep It Clear*, [summary](https://dev.walkboston.org/?p=1654))
34. **Open data on complaints and tickets.** Publish snow-related 311 requests and sidewalk citations by location and date, so patterns show: blocks reported every storm, and areas with no reports at all.
    - Somerville already publishes 311 service data ([Somerville services, data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/somerville-services)); over 400 complaints and at least 200 fines after one February 2025 storm ([CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/boston/news/somerville-citations-for-icy-sidewalks))
35. **Check where people cross, not only where people complain.** Enforcement that waits for complaints misses blocks where no one calls. After each storm, inspect corners, curb ramps and bus stops on the busiest routes.
    - Enforcement personnel for Sec. 12-8 include DPW, Inspectional Services and Traffic and Parking (Code of Ordinances, Sec. 1-11)
36. **Adopt a stop or a ramp.** Let neighbors who already clear a bus stop or corner sign up, so the City knows which stops are covered and can thank them. *(Card: Help a neighbor)*
37. **Treat small storms like big ones.** Residents tell PTAC that light snowfalls do more damage to sidewalks than big storms. With no snow emergency and no alert, fewer people shovel, the snow gets packed down and refreezes, and that is where dangerous ice builds up. Send a sidewalk reminder by City Alert for every snowfall that sticks, not only for emergencies, and inspect after small storms too. *(Cards: Ice, On time)*
    - Sec. 12-8 applies after any "weather event," whatever its size (Code of Ordinances, Sec. 12-8(a)); source of the observation: resident feedback to PTAC
38. **A reminder before the first storm.** Residents say the first December storm is when most people relearn the rules. Run a sidewalk campaign in November, before it snows: City Alert, social media, mailers with the tax or water bill, and signs in squares and at bus stops. *(Card: Get ready)*
    - Source: resident feedback to PTAC

## Proposals: data-driven clearing

Today the City has a priority order with no times, enforcement that waits for complaints, and no published results. These proposals set a target for each part of the network, measure against it, and publish the results.

39. **A clearing deadline for each layer.** Set a target time, counted from when the snow stops, for streets, bus stops and curb ramps, City-owned sidewalks and the Community Path. Private sidewalks already have one: 6 daylight hours. This puts numbers on proposal 30.
    - Eight-step priority order with no timing ([City snow page](https://www.somervillema.gov/snow))
40. **One map of everything that must be cleared.** A single geographic file of every bus stop (with MBTA stop ID and who clears it), every corner curb ramp (marked commercial or residential, since only businesses must clear ramps), every City-owned sidewalk stretch and every Community Path segment.
    - Commercial curb ramp duty (Code of Ordinances, Sec. 12-8(a)(1)); MBTA-cleared routes ([MBTA: bus service during winter storms](https://www.mbta.com/winter-weather/bus-service-during-winter-storms))
41. **Record when each item is cleared.** Crews log each bus stop, ramp or path segment from a phone as they finish it, with a time and a photo. Boston Parks did this with a simple form feeding a dashboard: inspection time fell from 30 hours to 6 per storm, violations on City-owned property fell from 150–200 per storm to 8, and over 60% of parks were cleared within 10 hours instead of 10 days. Plow GPS can come later.
    - [Boston Analytics Team: Snow Removal Dashboard](https://www.boston.gov/departments/analytics-team/snow-removal-dashboard); public plow data in [New York (PlowNYC)](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/City-Government/DSNY-PlowNYC-Data/rmhc-afj9) and [Philadelphia](https://www.phila.gov/2021-02-17-city-launches-interactive-map-to-track-and-monitor-treatment-of-roads-during-snow-events/)
42. **A fixed sample, checked after every storm.** Inspect the same set of corners, ramps and bus stops 24 and 48 hours after each storm, so the City sees conditions where nobody complains. This gives proposal 35 a method.
43. **Link the results to the weather.** Record snowfall, temperature and freeze-thaw for each storm next to the clearing and complaint data, to test what residents report: that small storms leave more ice than big ones (proposal 37).
44. **Follow each ticket to a cleared sidewalk.** Track how long a ticketed address stays uncleared, and whether DPW clears it under Sec. 12-8(c). This measures question 5.
45. **A public report each spring.** Time to clear each layer, blocks that fail every storm, how the pilot area compared with the rest of the city (proposal 31), complaints and tickets (proposal 34), and fall injuries and EMS calls from data partners (proposal 33).
46. **A PTAC partnership.** PTAC volunteers can audit 30 to 50 fixed points (key corners and bus stops) 24 and 48 hours after each storm this winter, with photos and times, and share the results with the City. One winter of this would be the first local evidence Somerville has, and a baseline for the City's own measurements.
    - 311 service data the City already publishes ([Somerville services, data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/somerville-services))

## City: data for charts and maps

What PTAC found in public data, and what's missing, is in [data-sources.md](data-sources.md).

47. **Why did sidewalk reports jump?** "Snow/sidewalk not shoveled" reports per inch of Boston snowfall were 6–15 from 2015 to 2022, then 51–72 in 2024/25 and 2025/26 (3,182 reports last winter). Was it the 311 app, publicity, new request types or enforcement? We don't want to show it as "people shovel less" if it isn't.
    - [311 Service, Information, and Feedback Requests](https://data.somervillema.gov/d/4pyi-uqq6); snowfall from NOAA via [ACIS](https://www.rcc-acis.org/)
48. **Snow emergencies.** Is there a list of declared snow emergencies since 2015, with start and end times? It would let us compare storms with and without an emergency (proposal 37).
49. **The 22 blue lights.** Which intersections have them? A map would help new residents learn the signal.
50. **Curb ramps.** Does the City have a GIS layer of curb ramps (for example from the ADA transition plan)? Neither the City nor MassDOT publishes one. See proposal 40.
51. **Tickets and liens.** Sidewalk-snow tickets issued, and clean-and-lien jobs done, per winter and ward. 311 shows complaints only.
52. **Which bus stop list?** The City says it clears 281 bus stops; MBTA lists 207 stops in Somerville. Which stops does the City count, and are its 4 routes public?
53. **ADA survey.** In the 2023 ADA Community Survey data, what does the 0–10 `sidewalks` column measure?
    - [Americans with Disabilities Act Community Survey Results](https://data.somervillema.gov/d/usj9-kz3f)

54. **Teen shoveling contact.** The City's February 2026 snow emergency notices give the Council on Aging at 617-625-6600 x2300; the guide uses x2321. Which is current?
    - [Snow emergency notice, Feb 21, 2026](https://www.somervillema.gov/news/somerville-declares-snow-emergency-effective-sunday-february-22-12-pm-noon)

55. **Traffic on Somerville streets.** Does the City have hourly traffic counts or a data-sharing deal (Waze for Cities or similar) that shows when cars are back on the streets during and after a storm? The replays use bus speed between timing stops as a stand-in.
56. **Parking revenue by day.** Daily parking meter and ParkMobile revenue, ideally by area, for the storms since 2016. It would show what a storm and a snow emergency cost the City and local businesses.
57. **Parking tickets in a snow emergency.** Parking tickets issued per day (snow emergency, hydrant, 20 feet of an intersection), the money collected, and the tow fees. The open-data citations cover moving violations only.
