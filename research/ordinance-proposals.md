# Sidewalk Snow Rules: Simpler, Clearer, More Accessible

Somerville Pedestrian & Transit Advisory Committee (PTAC) · Nov 3, 2026

Copy of the PTAC Claude Doc ("Proposal" tab). The live doc is the working version: https://claude.ai/artifact/7vG43e3w32sGB4efaCwyZE. The chart appears here as a table.

Dear Members of the City Council,

On behalf of the Somerville Pedestrian and Transit Advisory Committee (PTAC), thank you for your continued attention to walking and transit in our city. In March 2025, PTAC wrote to Mayor Ballantyne, with copies to each of you, about the condition of our sidewalks in winter. We write now with a specific request.

We respectfully ask the Council to amend Somerville's sidewalk snow ordinances so that they are simpler to follow, clear about every duty, and more protective of residents with disabilities at the places people most often get stuck: corners, bus stops and ice. Today the rules are hard to follow and harder to enforce. Complaints to 311 peak two to three days after the snow stops, long after the deadline, and nothing in the code makes sure a ticketed sidewalk is ever cleared.

## Why it matters

The deadline is measured in hours, but the danger lasts for days, and the people it strands are the ones who can least avoid it: wheelchair users, older residents, parents with strollers, and everyone walking to a bus.

## What we ask

### 1. Simplify the code

- **One deadline, the same as Boston.** 3 hours of daylight after the snow stops; snow that stops at night is due 3 hours after sunrise. The City states each storm's deadline as a clock time. ([details](#sec-12-8-the-deadline))
- **One name on every ticket.** The owner of record, found in seconds in City records. No investigating who is in charge, and no appeals over the wrong person. ([details](#sec-12-8-definitions-and-who-is-responsible))
- **Tickets without judgment calls.** Two fine levels read from City records, no offense history to track, no warnings to weigh, and a flat fee when the City has to clear. ([details](#sec-1-11-fines-inspections-and-follow-up))
- **Plain words for what is already intended.** Define sidewalk, storm and clear path; name contractors, downspouts and space savers instead of leaving them to interpretation. ([Sec. 12-9](#sec-12-9-snow-into-streets-bike-lanes-and-bus-stops), [12-2](#sec-12-2-water-and-ice-from-buildings), [12-18](#sec-12-18-space-savers-and-objects-in-the-path))

### 2. Clear duties for everyone

- **Owners** open the path by the deadline, keep it open until the ice is gone, and reopen it after refreezes and plows. No lease can make a tenant shovel or pay. ([details](#sec-12-8-the-path))
- **The City** clears its own sidewalks, bus stops and ramps on a schedule, re-inspects ticketed sidewalks within 24 hours, clears and bills what stays blocked, names one official in charge, and publishes the results. ([details](#new-sec-12-8a-the-citys-own-sidewalks-bus-stops-and-ramps))
- **Builders and absent owners** keep sidewalks and detours clear as a condition of every permit; vacant property files a winter contact, or the City clears it and bills the owner. ([details](#sec-12-8-construction-sites-and-vacant-property))

### 3. Expand accessibility protections

- **A path a wheelchair can use.** The full walking width, never under 48 inches, down to bare pavement, with ice treated within 2 daylight hours. ([details](#sec-12-8-the-path))
- **Open corners and bus stops.** Every corner property clears its curb ramp, its raised warning surface and an opening to the crosswalk; the path reaches the curb at bus stops; construction detours are signed and detectable by cane. ([details](#sec-12-8-corners-crosswalks-bus-stops-hydrants-and-drains))
- **ADA standards in winter.** The 8-by-5-foot boarding area at bus stops, signal push buttons kept reachable, accessible parking cleared, notices in accessible formats, and a 311 fast lane to the City's ADA Coordinator when someone is stuck. ([details](#new-sec-12-8a-the-citys-own-sidewalks-bus-stops-and-ramps))
- **Help instead of fines** for eligible seniors and residents with disabilities who can't shovel. ([details](#sec-12-8-extensions-being-away-and-help))

## The proposed text

Every change we propose, written as ordinance text for the City's legislative counsel to put into final form: the sidewalk rule (Sec. 12-8), the City's own duties (new Sec. 12-8A), notices and data (new Sec. 12-8B), and the related changes to Sec. 12-2, 12-9, 12-18 and 1-11. Attachment A explains each change from today's text and who it affects.

**Sec. 12-8. Snow and ice on sidewalks.**

**(a) Purpose, definitions and who is responsible.**

(1) Sidewalks, curb ramps, crosswalks and bus stops are part of the city's transportation network. This section keeps them passable in winter for everyone, including people who use wheelchairs, walkers, canes or strollers. The City's goal is that they are fully passable within 24 hours after each event.

(2) In this section: "Sidewalk" means the paved way between the property line and the curb, including curb ramps, the landing at the top of a ramp, and driveway crossings. "Winter weather event" means snow, sleet or freezing rain, or ice formed when meltwater refreezes; it ends when snow, sleet or freezing rain stops falling in the city, at the time stated in the City's notice under Section 12-8B or, without a notice, when it stops. "Clear path" means a continuous path along the whole frontage, the full width of the walking surface, never less than 48 inches (or the whole sidewalk where it is narrower), down to bare pavement, connecting to the paths of neighboring properties, curb ramps and bus stops.

(3) The responsible person is the owner of record of the land or building, as shown in the City's assessing records. Tickets, bills and liens under this section go to the owner. The owner may hire a contractor, but no lease may make a tenant shovel or pay a ticket or clearing fee. The duty covers every sidewalk the property abuts, including both sides of a corner lot.

**(b) Deadlines.**

(1) The responsible person shall open the clear path within three hours after the event ends, counting only hours between sunrise and sunset. If the event ends after dark, the three hours start at sunrise.

(2) The City's notice under Section 12-8B states each event's deadline as a clock time.

(3) These deadlines apply after every event, whether or not a snow emergency is declared, and on weekends and holidays. Ice that forms later from melting and refreezing, snow that drifts back, and snow a plow pushes onto a cleared path or curb ramp are new events, counted from when they form or the plow passes.

(4) The City may extend a deadline only by a public notice under Section 12-8B, issued before the deadline passes, that states the new deadline as a date and time.

(5) Being away does not excuse the duty. A responsible person who will be away shall arrange for someone else to clear.

(6) During an event that lasts more than 12 hours, the responsible person shall also clear and treat the path within three hours after sunrise each day it continues.

**(c) City clearing, re-inspection and cost.** An inspector who finds a violation of this section shall issue a ticket to the owner; there are no warnings, except for a property registered under subsection (e). The inspector shall re-inspect within 24 hours. A frontage still not clear gets a new ticket, as a new offense under Section 1-11(a). If the clear path is not open 24 hours after the deadline, or 12 hours after a ticket, the Commissioner of Public Works shall clear it, starting with bus routes, squares, school walking routes and corners. The owner pays, in addition to the ticket, a flat clearing fee per frontage and per curb ramp that the City sets to match its average cost; an unpaid fee becomes a lien on the property.

**(d) Collections.** The city treasurer with the approval of the mayor may in civil actions prosecute and adjust claims inuring to the city under the provisions of this section. (Unchanged.)

**(e) Snow assistance.** The City shall run a snow assistance program. An owner-occupant who is 65 or older or has a disability, and whose household income is below a limit the City sets, may register by December 1. The City or its volunteers clear registered frontages, curb ramps included, and a registered owner is not fined while registered. The City shall keep a public sign-up for neighbors who adopt a curb ramp, a bus stop or a registered neighbor's frontage.

**(f) The clear path.** Remove snow and ice down to bare pavement. Ice that cannot be removed shall be treated with sand or a de-icer by the deadline and removed as soon as it can be. Treating ice does not replace removing snow. Ice on a sidewalk or curb ramp shall be treated within two daylight hours after the event ends or the ice forms. Keep the clear path open and treated until the snow and ice are gone.

**(g) Corners, crosswalks and bus stops.** (1) The responsible person for a property abutting a curb ramp, including each ramp at a corner lot, shall clear the ramp, its landing, its raised warning surface, and a clear area at least 48 inches square at its bottom in the street, by the same deadline and to the same standard as the sidewalk. (2) Where a plow bank separates a curb ramp or corner from a crosswalk, the responsible person shall cut an opening at least 48 inches wide through it to the street, in line with the crosswalk. (3) At a bus stop, the clear path continues to the curb at the bus stop sign, at least 48 inches wide. (4) The responsible person shall keep a 3-foot circle clear around any hydrant on its frontage, by the same deadline as the sidewalk.

**(h) Where snow may not go.** No person, including a contractor clearing a driveway, parking lot or sidewalk, may place snow or ice on any sidewalk, curb ramp, crosswalk or bus stop, within 3 feet of a hydrant, on a storm drain, against a pole with a pedestrian push button, or in an accessible parking space or its access aisle. Snow goes onto the owner's own property, or the owner has it hauled away. It may also go into the tree strip where it does not narrow the clear path or block the view of people crossing at a corner.

**(i) Contact for tenants.** Each October, the owner of a rental building shall give occupants, in writing, the name and phone number of the owner or the contractor the owner has hired to clear the sidewalk and curb ramps, so they can report a missed walk during a storm.

**(j) Fines.** Each ticket under this section is $150 for an owner-occupied home (one with the residential tax exemption) and $300 for any other property, for untreated ice, and while a building, demolition or sidewalk-opening permit is open, issued to the owner, in accordance with Section 1-11. Each day a violation continues is a new offense.

**(k) Construction and vacant property.** (1) Every permit to place a dumpster, obstruct or open a sidewalk, or build or demolish, includes this condition: keep the sidewalk, or the permitted detour, clear by the deadlines of this section; breaking it violates this section and the permit. (2) A permit that closes a sidewalk shall provide an accessible detour at least 48 inches wide, signed before the closure and edged with barriers a cane can detect, which the permit holder keeps clear of snow and ice. (3) The clearing fee under subsection (c) is drawn from the permit's deposit or bond, and the holder restores it within 5 business days. (4) After 3 tickets under this section on one permit, Inspectional Services may hold the project's next inspection until the sidewalk is clear and the tickets are paid. (5) By November 1, the owner of vacant land or a vacant building shall file with the City the name and 24-hour phone number of the person who clears it; if no one is filed, or a deadline is missed, the City clears the frontage after every event for the rest of the winter and charges the clearing fee each time.

**(l) Accessible parking.** The owner of a property with a parking lot shall clear its accessible parking spaces, their access aisles and the route to the entrance whenever the lot is cleared.

**Sec. 12-8A. City sidewalks, bus stops and curb ramps.**

**(a) City property.** The City shall meet the deadlines and standard of Section 12-8 on every sidewalk abutting City land or buildings, including schools, parks, libraries, fire stations and parking lots, and on the bridges and Community Path segments it maintains.

**(b) Bus stops.** The City shall clear each bus stop it is responsible for, including the path to the curb, a boarding area at least 8 feet deep from the curb and 5 feet wide, and any shelter, within 24 hours after a winter weather event ends, and the stops it names as priority within 12 hours. While an event lasts, stops on routes that are running shall be kept passable at least once each morning and afternoon.

**(c) Curb ramps with no abutter.** The City shall clear curb ramps and crosswalk openings that no private property abuts, and reopen any curb ramp its plows bury, within 24 hours after the event ends or the plow passes.

**(d) Ice melt and pre-treatment.** The City shall offer residents free sand or ice melt at published locations, announce it each November, and pre-treat bus stops, curb ramps and City sidewalks on priority routes before forecast ice.

**(e) Winter plan.** By November 1 each year, the Commissioner of Public Works shall publish a winter plan: the clearing order; a target time for each layer (streets, bus stops, curb ramps, City sidewalks, Community Path); and a map of every bus stop and curb ramp showing who clears it.

**(f) Clearing log.** City crews shall record the time each bus stop, curb ramp and City frontage is cleared.

**(g) Lead official.** The Mayor shall name one official to lead sidewalk snow clearing across departments, responsible for the winter plan, public education, enforcement data and the report under Section 12-8B.

**(h) Agreements.** The City shall seek written agreements with the MBTA, the Massachusetts Department of Transportation and the Department of Conservation and Recreation on who clears their bus stops, station entrances and sidewalks in the city, and by when, including how stops stay passable while buses run during a storm, and shall publish them.

**(i) Push buttons and accessible parking.** The City shall keep pedestrian push buttons at signals reachable from the cleared curb ramp, and clear accessible parking spaces on its streets and in its lots, including snow emergency lots, within 24 hours after the event ends.

**Sec. 12-8B. Notices, reporting and open data.**

**(a) Deadline notice.** For every winter weather event, the City shall announce, by City Alert and on its website, the time the event ended and the clearing deadline as a clock time, in the languages in which it publishes its snow rules and in formats accessible to people with disabilities. Extensions under Section 12-8(b)(4) shall be announced the same way.

**(b) Season guide.** Each November, the City shall send every household and business the sidewalk rules, with the water or tax bill or by mail, including photographs of acceptable and unacceptable clearing; the duties of owners and permit holders; suitable tools and de-icers; the snow assistance and teen shoveler programs; and how to hire and oversee a snow contractor.

**(c) 311 outcomes.** A 311 request about an uncleared sidewalk shall be closed with its outcome: clear on inspection, ticket, cleared by the City, or not found.

**(d) Open data.** From November through April, the City shall publish weekly on its open data portal: inspections and tickets under Sections 12-2, 12-8, 12-9 and 12-18, with date, time, street and ward but no house number; City clearing and liens under Section 12-8(c); the clearing log under Section 12-8A(f); and each declared snow emergency with its start and end times.

**(e) Annual report.** By June 30 each year, the Commissioner of Public Works and the Director of Inspectional Services shall report to the City Council on the preceding winter: complaints, inspections, tickets, City clearings and liens, and the time taken to clear each layer under Section 12-8A, by ward and by event, alongside each event's snowfall. The report may be included in the annual report under Section 12-116.6.

**(f) Blocked accessible routes.** 311 shall offer a request type for a blocked curb ramp, crosswalk opening, bus stop or path that keeps a person with a disability from leaving home or reaching transit. These requests go to the City's ADA Coordinator and shall be answered within 12 hours.

**(g) Accessible formats.** Notices, the season guide and open data under this section shall be provided in formats accessible to people with disabilities.

**Sec. 12-2. Water run-off, ice from buildings and washing cars.**

**(a)** No building, awning or other projection may shed or discharge water onto a sidewalk, curb ramp or bus stop. This includes roofs, gutters and downspouts, and discharge from sump pumps, heat pumps, furnaces, boilers and air conditioners. Water shall be piped under the sidewalk to the street or a drain, or onto the owner's own ground.

**(b)** Unchanged (washing cars, and other water flowing onto streets and sidewalks).

**(c)** Ice formed by water from a building is the owner's to treat and remove under Section 12-8, whenever it forms.

**(d)** The owner shall promptly remove snow and ice that could fall onto a sidewalk from a roof, awning or sign, and mark the danger until it is removed.

**(e)** Whoever violates this section shall be fined in accordance with Section 1-11.

**Sec. 12-9. Snow and ice placed in streets.**

**(a)** No person, including a contractor clearing a driveway, parking lot or sidewalk, shall place snow or ice from private land onto a street, bike lane, bus stop, crosswalk or curb ramp.

**(b)** On a street only, and never in a bike lane, bus stop, crosswalk or curb ramp, snow or ice may be broken up and spread no more than three inches thick, in daylight, when the air temperature at that time is above 40 degrees Fahrenheit.

**(c)** A ticket under this section goes to the owner of the property the snow or ice came from, in accordance with Section 1-11.

**Sec. 12-18. Obstacles in streets and sidewalks.** Retitled from "Obstacles in streets or sidewalks at night"; subsections (a) through (d) unchanged. Add:

**(e)** No person may place a chair, cone or any other object in a street to hold a parking space. The City may remove and discard it at once. Each object is a separate offense.

**(f)** No object, including a trash or recycling bin, may stand in a clear path, curb ramp or bus stop required by Section 12-8. On collection day, bins go at the curb edge in front of any snowbank.

**Sec. 1-11. Penalties and violations (amendments).**

**In subsection (a),** strike "except that snow removal violations shall be counted from October 1 through May 1 of the following year", which the flat fines below make unnecessary.

**In the table in subsection (b),** replace the rows for Sec. 12-8 and Sec. 12-9 with one row for Water run-off and ice from buildings (Sec. 12-2), Snow and ice on sidewalks (Sec. 12-8) and Snow and ice placed in streets (Sec. 12-9): $150 per offense for an owner-occupied dwelling with the residential tax exemption; $300 per offense for any other property, for untreated ice, and while a building, demolition or sidewalk-opening permit is open on the property. Enforcing personnel: DPW commissioner; inspectional services; traffic and parking; police. Rename the Sec. 12-18 row "Obstacles in streets and sidewalks" (fine unchanged).

**Add a subsection on snow and ice tickets:** (1) A ticket under Sections 12-2, 12-8 or 12-9 names the owner of record, from the City's assessing records, and is mailed to the owner's tax-bill address; the inspector need not find out who lives in, rents or manages the property. (2) An inspector who finds a violation shall issue a ticket. There are no warnings, except for a property registered in the snow assistance program under Section 12-8(e). (3) Within 24 hours after the deadline for every event that leaves snow or ice on the ground, inspectors shall check bus routes, squares and school walking routes, whether or not anyone complained. (4) Tickets and notices include how to pay, appeal and get help, in the languages in which the City publishes its snow rules. (5) Fines and clearing charges under Section 12-8 shall fund the snow assistance program and City clearing of curb ramps and bus stops, subject to appropriation.

## Attachment A: The changes, section by section

Each change quotes the code as it reads today, the proposed text, and the reason. Each change ends with who it affects. Staff costs are not estimated here; the City should price them before the Council votes.

### Sec. 12-8: definitions and who is responsible

Name the terms the rule depends on, and put one name on every ticket: the owner of record.

**Purpose**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Sidewalks, curb ramps, crosswalks and bus stops are part of the city's transportation network. This section keeps them passable in winter for everyone, including people who use wheelchairs, walkers, canes or strollers. The City's goal is that they are fully passable within 24 hours after each event."
- *Why:* Gives inspectors and the Council a test for gray areas: can a person using a wheelchair get through?

**Sidewalk**

- *Today:* Not defined.
- *Proposed:* "Sidewalk: the paved way between the property line and the curb, including curb ramps, the landing at the top of a ramp, and driveway crossings."
- *Why:* WalkBoston's statewide review names corners and ramps as the worst trouble spots in winter. Today the code never mentions ramps for homes; naming them in the definition removes doubt.

**Weather event**

- *Today:* "following the conclusion of a weather event" (not defined).
- *Proposed:* "Winter weather event: snow, sleet or freezing rain, or ice formed when meltwater refreezes. The event ends when snow, sleet or freezing rain stops falling in the city, at the time stated in the City's notice under Sec. 12-8B, or, without a notice, when it stops."
- *Why:* Freezing rain or sleet fell in 22 of 44 snow periods since 2016. A published end time ends arguments over when the clock started.

**Clear path**

- *Today:* "a path of travel with a minimum width of thirty-six inches"
- *Proposed:* "Clear path: a continuous path along the whole frontage, the full width of the walking surface, never less than 48 inches (or the whole sidewalk where it is narrower), down to bare pavement, connecting to the paths of neighboring properties, curb ramps and bus stops."
- *Why:* Width explained under "The path". "Continuous" and "connecting" stop the gap-at-the-property-line problem.

**Who is responsible**

- *Today:* "A person, including an owner, occupant, tenant, or agent, in charge of land or a building"
- *Proposed:* "The owner of record of the land or building, as shown in the City's assessing records. Tickets, bills and liens under this section go to the owner. The owner may hire a contractor, but no lease may make a tenant shovel or pay a ticket or clearing fee."
- *Why:* Finding out who controls a property (owner, tenant, manager, business) takes an investigation the City can't afford on a $100 ticket, and a ticket to the wrong person starts an appeal. The owner is in the City's records in seconds, the lien already attaches to the property, and no lease can pass the shoveling, a ticket or a fee to a tenant, as PTAC's March 2025 letter asked. A tenant may be unable to shovel, and has no say over a contractor who misses. State law aims the sidewalk penalty at "the owner of abutting property or his agent" (M.G.L. c. 40 §21(3)). Washington, DC lets an owner delegate in writing but keeps the owner liable. This extends the State Sanitary Code's rule to the sidewalk: it keeps shared exits with the landlord and lets a lease shift only a tenant's own exit, in writing ([105 CMR 410.260](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/105-CMR-410-260)).

**Which sidewalks**

- *Today:* "an abutting sidewalk"
- *Proposed:* "every sidewalk the property abuts, including both sides of a corner lot"
- *Why:* "An abutting sidewalk" is singular; naming both sides of a corner lot removes an easy argument on appeal.

**Contact for tenants**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Each October, the owner of a rental building shall give occupants, in writing, the name and phone number of the owner or the contractor the owner has hired to clear the sidewalk and curb ramps, so they can report a missed walk during a storm."
- *Why:* Nothing in the code or a standard lease tells tenants who clears. Since the owner gets the ticket, a written note each fall tells tenants whom to call when a walk is missed.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** no change; they get their own tickets, as today.
- **Tenants:** never made to shovel, ticketed or charged.
- **Landlords and other owners:** shovel or hire someone for every rental, pay every ticket, and send tenants one notice each October. The cost may show up in rents.
- **City staff:** no more investigating who is in charge, and fewer appeals over the wrong person.

### Sec. 12-8: the deadline

Keep today's rule and change one number: **3 hours of daylight** after the snow stops, the same as Boston. If the snow stops after dark, the 3 hours start at sunrise. The City announces each storm's deadline as a clock time, so no one has to work it out.

**The deadline**

- *Today:* "may not ... allow it to remain ... for more than six hours between sunrise and sunset following the conclusion of a weather event"
- *Proposed:* "... for more than three hours between sunrise and sunset after the event ends." In practice: 3 hours after the snow stops, or 3 hours after sunrise if it stops after dark.
- *Why:* Boston's rule, next door: one number, and one rule for residents, landlords and contractors who work in both cities. Today's 6 hours is twice Boston's. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for 4; matching Boston is simpler to explain.

**A clock time in every notice**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The City's notice under Sec. 12-8B states each event's deadline as a clock time."
- *Why:* No one should have to compute sunrise. The City already rounds its own notices (12:24 became 12:30; 12:28 became "1 p.m."), so it should simply state the time.

**Every event**

- *Today:* Applies to any "weather event".
- *Proposed:* Keep, and add: "These deadlines apply after every event, whether or not a snow emergency is declared, and on weekends and holidays."
- *Why:* Residents tell PTAC that small storms leave the worst ice because people skip them; the code should say plainly that every storm counts.

**Long snowfalls**

- *Today:* None: the clock starts only when the event ends.
- *Proposed:* "During an event that lasts more than 12 hours, the responsible person shall also clear and treat the path within three hours after sunrise each day it continues."
- *Why:* Two days of light snow set no deadline at all today, while schools and shops stay open. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for clearing during long, light snowfalls, when packed snow turns to ice.

**How others set it:**

| City | Deadline after the snow stops |
| --- | --- |
| Boston | 3 hours; 3 hours after sunrise if it stops overnight |
| New York City | 4 hours (stops 7 a.m.–5 p.m.); 14 hours (5–9 p.m.); by 11 a.m. (9 p.m.–7 a.m.) |
| Worcester | 10 hours |
| Washington, DC | the first 8 hours of daylight |
| Cambridge | 12 hours after daytime snow; by 1 p.m. after overnight snow (2012 code text) |

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** clear sooner than today (about 3 hours sooner for snow that stops at night, more for snow that stops in the afternoon), and again each morning during storms longer than 12 hours.
- **City staff:** one rule shared with Boston; state each deadline as a clock time in the notice; more inspections during long storms.

### Sec. 12-8: the path

Widen the path to the full walking width, never under 48 inches, make bare pavement the rule and sand the stopgap, and make the duty last until the ice is gone, including ice that forms later and snow the plow pushes back.

**Width**

- *Today:* "a path of travel with a minimum width of thirty-six inches"
- *Proposed:* "the full width of the walking surface, never less than 48 inches (or the whole sidewalk where it is narrower)"
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for the entire width, as in Minneapolis. 36 inches is the bare minimum for one wheelchair, with no room to pass or turn. Federal guidelines set 48 inches for new and altered public sidewalks (PROWAG R302.2, 2023); 36 inches is the ADA minimum for a walking surface. Boston asks for 42 inches; Worcester and New York City require 4 ft. Somerville's own code already keeps 4 ft clear of newsracks (Sec. 12-69(1)). 98% of Somerville's mapped sidewalk miles are 4 ft or wider.

**Finish**

- *Today:* "clear the sidewalk to its surface, or treat it with sand or other suitable material where clearing to the surface is impractical"
- *Proposed:* "Remove snow and ice down to bare pavement. Ice that cannot be removed shall be treated with sand or a de-icer by the deadline and removed as soon as it can be. Treating ice does not replace removing snow."
- *Why:* Today a sprinkle of sand over packed snow can pass as compliance. A thin layer left behind freezes into the worst ice.

**Keeping it clear**

- *Today:* None: the duty ends at the deadline.
- *Proposed:* "Keep the clear path open and treated until the snow and ice are gone. Ice that forms later from melting and refreezing, and snow that drifts back, are new events under subsection (b), counted from when they form."
- *Why:* Reports keep coming for a median of 6 days after the snow. Melt-and-refreeze ice causes slips long after the storm.

**Plow re-burial**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Snow a plow pushes onto a cleared path or curb ramp is a new event under subsection (b), counted from when the plow passes."
- *Why:* Residents ask whether the clock restarts. This answers yes, and gives them a fair deadline.

**Where snow goes**

- *Today:* "may not place snow or ice ... on an abutting sidewalk"
- *Proposed:* "No person, including a contractor clearing a driveway, parking lot or sidewalk, may place snow or ice on any sidewalk, curb ramp, crosswalk or bus stop, within 3 feet of a hydrant, on a storm drain, against a pole with a pedestrian push button, or in an accessible parking space or its access aisle. Snow goes onto the owner's own property, or the owner has it hauled away. It may also go into the tree strip where it does not narrow the clear path or block the view of people crossing at a corner."
- *Why:* Today the ban covers only one's own abutting sidewalk, not contractors who push lot snow across a walk. Piles at corners hide children and wheelchair users from drivers. Snow piled against a signal pole puts the crossing button out of reach; federal guidelines place it within 5 feet of the ramp (PROWAG R307).

**Ice first**

- *Today:* None: ice has the same deadline as snow.
- *Proposed:* "Ice on a sidewalk or curb ramp shall be treated with sand or a de-icer within 2 daylight hours after the event ends or the ice forms."
- *Why:* Ice causes the worst falls. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked that enforcement on ice start sooner and cost more; members found ice on sidewalks up to 7 days after the snow in winter 2024–25.

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** wider shoveling (most mapped sidewalks are 5 to 7 ft); sand or salt on hand; a return trip after refreezes and plows; snow piled on their own property or hauled away at their cost, which matters most on small lots. Parking-lot contractors can't push snow across walks; some will haul it.
- **Tenants and everyone on foot:** a wider, safer path.
- **City staff:** fewer hydrants and drains to dig out.

### Sec. 12-8: corners, crosswalks, bus stops, hydrants and drains

A cleared sidewalk is useless if the corner is a snowbank. Extend the ramp duty to every corner property, add an opening to the crosswalk, and say who clears each bus stop.

**Curb ramps**

- *Today:* (a)(1): only "a building with exclusively commercial units or a building with ground floor commercial units abutting an accessible curb ramp shall also clear the curb ramp to provide ... clear and direct access to the crosswalk"
- *Proposed:* "The responsible person for any property abutting a curb ramp, including each ramp at a corner lot, shall clear the ramp, its landing, its raised warning surface, and a clear area at least 48 inches square at its bottom in the street, by the same deadline and to the same standard as the sidewalk." Owners who qualify get help under (e) instead of a fine.
- *Why:* Ramps sit at corners, and many corners are homes, where the ordinance requires no one to clear them. The City's snow page already tells residents and businesses they are "responsible for" clearing "accessible ramps that abut their property", a duty the ordinance places only on businesses. New York City requires corner owners to clear their ramps; Boston and Cambridge tell owners on their City pages that they must; Worcester asks. Ord. 2025-25 (Dec 2025) covered businesses only; this finishes the job. The raised warning domes tell people who are blind where the street begins, and federal guidelines call for a 48-inch-square clear area at the bottom of every ramp (PROWAG R305, R304.2.4).

**Crosswalk opening**

- *Today:* Implied only for businesses ("clear and direct access to the crosswalk").
- *Proposed:* "Where a plow bank separates the ramp or corner from a crosswalk, cut an opening at least 48 inches wide through it to the street, in line with the crosswalk."
- *Why:* A clear ramp that ends at a 3-foot plow bank still traps a wheelchair. Boston's code (2017 text) required a 42-inch path at curb ramps "from the sidewalk to the street"; Brookline and Cambridge ask for crosswalk openings.

**Bus stops (abutters)**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "At a bus stop, the clear path continues to the curb at the bus stop sign, at least 48 inches wide."
- *Why:* Riders step off into snowbanks. In Somerville the MBTA clears stops only on routes 77, 104 and 109, within 24 hours; the City clears its 281 stops third in its clearing order, after streets and City lots, with no time set. New York City and Ann Arbor make abutters clear the walk at bus stops.

**Bus stops (City)**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* See new Sec. 12-8A: the City clears every stop it is responsible for within 24 hours and publishes which stops are its job and which are the MBTA's.
- *Why:* The City counts 281 stops it clears; the MBTA lists 207 stops in Somerville. Which stops are whose job is not published, so a rider can't tell who to call, or when to expect it.

**Hydrants**

- *Today:* None. The City's snow page says crews shovel out hydrants and welcomes help from residents who are able "(3 feet in all directions)".
- *Proposed:* "No person may place snow within 3 feet of a hydrant. The responsible person shall keep a 3-foot circle clear around any hydrant on its frontage, by the same deadline as the sidewalk."
- *Why:* Longmeadow's bylaw (2017 text) requires the adjacent owner to clear a hydrant within 24 hours; New York City requires owners to clear around hydrants. Owners already clear the sidewalk beside the hydrant; the circle adds a few minutes of shoveling and spares City crews the digging.

**Storm drains**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "No person may place snow or ice on a storm drain."
- *Why:* Meltwater that can't drain refreezes across the ramp and crosswalk.

**Accessible parking**

- *Today:* None for snow.
- *Proposed:* "The owner of a property with a parking lot shall clear its accessible parking spaces, their access aisles and the route to the entrance whenever the lot is cleared. No person may pile snow in an accessible space or aisle."
- *Why:* Federal rules already require businesses to keep accessible features usable (28 CFR 36.211). The Justice Department's guidance says to clear accessible spaces "whenever plowing or clearing the rest of the parking area" and not to pile snow in them. Putting it in the code lets inspectors enforce it.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** corner homes clear their ramps and a crosswalk opening; the few at bus stops clear a 48-inch path to the curb; owners with a hydrant clear a 3-foot circle around it. Eligible seniors and owners with disabilities get help instead.
- **Landlords and other owners:** the same on their corners and stops (owners of commercial buildings already must clear ramps); owners of parking lots also clear accessible spaces, aisles and the route to the door when the lot is plowed.
- **City staff:** fewer ramps, bus stops and hydrants for City crews to clear.

### Sec. 12-8: construction sites and vacant property

Both are covered in principle today, since the duty falls on whoever is "in charge of land or a building". In practice no one is around to do it, and the permits that let a site use the sidewalk say nothing about winter. Tie the duty to the permit, and give vacant property a default: the City clears and bills.

**Winter permit condition**

- *Today:* The ordinance sets no winter conditions for dumpster (Sec. 12-77), obstruction (Sec. 12-12) or sidewalk-opening (Sec. 12-20) permits; any conditions are left to each permit.
- *Proposed:* "Every permit to place a dumpster, obstruct or open a sidewalk, or build or demolish, includes this condition: keep the sidewalk, or the permitted detour, clear by the deadlines of this section. Breaking it violates this section and the permit."
- *Why:* Sec. 12-77 already treats breaking a permit condition as a violation; this puts snow in the conditions.

**Sidewalk closures**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "A permit that closes a sidewalk shall provide an accessible detour at least 48 inches wide, signed before the closure and edged with barriers a cane can detect, which the permit holder keeps clear of snow and ice."
- *Why:* A closed sidewalk with an unplowed detour pushes walkers and wheelchair users into the street. Federal guidelines ask for the same signs and cane-detectable edges (PROWAG R303); PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for clearly marked, cleared detours.

**Paying for City clearing**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The clearing fee under subsection (c) is drawn from the permit's deposit or bond. The holder restores it within 5 business days."
- *Why:* The City gets paid without sending a bill. Sec. 12-20 already holds a $500 deposit and a $5,000 bond for sidewalk openings.

**Holding the permit**

- *Today:* Sec. 1-11(c): unpaid fines may block local licenses and permits.
- *Proposed:* "After 3 tickets under this section on one permit, Inspectional Services may hold the project's next inspection until the sidewalk is clear and the tickets are paid."
- *Why:* A delayed inspection costs a project far more than any ticket the state allows. The Law Department should confirm this use of the permit.

**Vacant land and buildings**

- *Today:* Covered as "land or a building", with no one to reach.
- *Proposed:* "By November 1, the owner of vacant land or a vacant building shall file with the City the name and 24-hour phone number of the person who clears it. If no one is filed, or a deadline is missed, the City clears the frontage after every event for the rest of the winter and charges the clearing fee each time."
- *Why:* No investigation and no chase: one filing, or the City does the work and the lien collects it.

**Who is affected**

- **Developers and vacant-property owners:** winter permit conditions, signed detours, deposit draws and $300 tickets; vacant owners file a winter contact or pay the clearing fee after each storm.
- **City staff:** add the condition to permits, track tickets per permit, and clear vacant frontages.

### Sec. 12-8: extensions, being away, and help

Announce extensions in public, say plainly that being away is no excuse, and pair a wider duty with real help for people who can't shovel.

**Extensions**

- *Today:* "or as otherwise extended by the city in its discretion"
- *Proposed:* "The City may extend a deadline only by a public notice under Sec. 12-8B, issued before the deadline passes, that states the new deadline as a date and time."
- *Why:* The code doesn't say how or where an extension is announced, so residents can't know whether one applies to them.

**Being away**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Being away does not excuse the duty. A responsible person who will be away shall arrange for someone else to clear."
- *Why:* Brookline and Cambridge already tell residents this (see Sources). Writing it in the code removes an argument on appeal.

**Help for those who can't**

- *Today:* None. The City runs a paid teen-shoveler program ($25, or $60 for a corner lot, in 2024/25).
- *Proposed:* "(e) The City shall run a snow assistance program. An owner-occupant who is 65 or older or has a disability, and whose household income is below a limit the City sets, may register by December 1. The City or its volunteers clear registered frontages, ramps included. A registered owner is not fined while registered."
- *Why:* A wider duty (corner ramps, a wider path, continuing ice) must not fall on residents who physically can't do it. Cambridge's City crews clear for homeowners over 60 or with a disability who meet income limits. Washington, DC does not fine residential owners who are 65 or older or have a disability.

**Volunteers**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall keep a public sign-up for neighbors who adopt a ramp, bus stop or a registered neighbor's frontage."
- *Why:* A public list lets the City know which ramps and stops are covered, send help where none is, and thank the volunteers.

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** arrange help before traveling. Eligible owners in the assistance program are cleared and not fined.
- **City staff:** post a notice for each extension and see fewer appeals; run a new assistance program (registration, crews or volunteers, a budget).

### Sec. 12-8(c): when the City clears and bills

A ticket doesn't clear ice. Turn "may clear" into a set time for re-inspection and City clearing, so every ticketed sidewalk ends up open.

**City clearing**

- *Today:* "Upon neglect or violation of the duty ... such duty may be performed by the superintendent of highways at the expense of the person liable"
- *Proposed:* "If the clear path is not open 24 hours after the deadline, or 12 hours after a ticket, the Commissioner of Public Works shall clear it, starting with bus routes, squares, school walking routes and corners."
- *Why:* "May" with no time sets no expectation: residents can't tell whether or when the City will step in. The City's own snow page gives two different start times. "Superintendent of highways" is an old title.

**Re-inspection**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "An inspector who issues a ticket shall re-inspect within 24 hours. A frontage still not clear gets a new ticket, as a new offense under Sec. 1-11(a)."
- *Why:* Ticketing (ISD) and clearing (DPW) sit in different departments, and the ordinance sets no handoff between them. Sec. 1-11(a) already counts each day as a new offense; this makes it happen.

**Cost**

- *Today:* "at the expense of the person liable ... and the city may place a lien against the property for expenses incurred"
- *Proposed:* "The owner pays a flat clearing fee per frontage and per curb ramp, set by the City to match its average cost, on top of the ticket. Unpaid fees become a municipal charges lien."
- *Why:* A flat fee is known in advance and needs no invoice per job; set it to cover the City's average cost. Unpaid fees become a lien, as Sec. 1-11(c) already allows.

**Collections**

- *Today:* (d) "The city treasurer with the approval of the mayor may in civil actions prosecute and adjust claims"
- *Proposed:* Keep unchanged.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** pay the City's flat clearing fee when its crews have to clear.
- **City staff:** return visits within 24 hours of a ticket; DPW crews clear more frontages; the fee recovers the cost.

### New Sec. 12-8A: the City's own sidewalks, bus stops and ramps

Residents have a clearing deadline; the City's own frontages, bus stops and ramps get none. Hold the City to the same standard and publish the plan.

**City property**

- *Today:* None. Sec. 12-8 names "a person ... in charge of land or a building"; whether it binds the City is unclear.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall meet the deadlines and standard of Sec. 12-8 on every sidewalk abutting City land or buildings, including schools, parks, libraries, fire stations and parking lots, and on the bridges and Community Path segments it maintains."
- *Why:* The rule residents follow should be the rule the City follows; today the ordinance is silent on City frontages. Washington, DC's code makes the city clear its own sidewalks and crosswalks within 8 daylight hours.

**Bus stops**

- *Today:* None. The snow page says the City clears 281 stops, "divided into 4 Routes", third in its clearing order, after streets and City lots.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall clear each bus stop it is responsible for (the path to the curb, a boarding area at least 8 feet deep from the curb and 5 feet wide, and any shelter) within 24 hours after the event ends, and stops it names as priority within 12 hours. While an event lasts, stops on routes that are running are kept passable at least once each morning and afternoon."
- *Why:* Riders need a time they can count on. The MBTA already commits to 24 hours for the stops it clears. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked that stops be clear whenever buses are running. The 8-by-5-foot boarding area is the federal standard for a bus stop (2010 ADA Standards 810.2.2; PROWAG R309).

**Ramps with no owner to clear them**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall clear curb ramps and crosswalk openings that no private property abuts, and reopen any ramp its plows bury, within 24 hours after the event ends or the plow passes."
- *Why:* The City's snow page lists curb cuts and intersection landings among the City's work, with no deadline and no list of which ones.

**A plan with times**

- *Today:* None. The snow page lists an eight-step clearing order with no times.
- *Proposed:* "By November 1 each year, the Commissioner of Public Works shall publish a winter plan: the clearing order, a target time for each layer (streets, bus stops, ramps, City sidewalks, Community Path), and a map of every bus stop and curb ramp showing who clears it."
- *Why:* Residents can plan around a schedule; today they can't tell whether a stop is late or not on any list.

**Proof of clearing**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Crews shall record the time each bus stop, ramp and City frontage is cleared."
- *Why:* Boston's parks crews did this with a simple form; inspection time per storm fell from 30 hours to 6 (Boston Analytics Team).

**State and MBTA property**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall seek written agreements with the MBTA, MassDOT and DCR on who clears their stops, stations and sidewalks in Somerville, and by when, and publish them."
- *Why:* The City lists State property last in its clearing order, with no contact. PTAC could not find who clears the sidewalks along McGrath Highway or the Green Line station entrances.

**Free ice melt and pre-treatment**

- *Today:* None in the code. 311 takes "Sand barrel" requests (500 since 2015).
- *Proposed:* "The City shall offer residents free sand or ice melt at published locations, announce it each November, and pre-treat bus stops, curb ramps and City sidewalks on priority routes before forecast ice."
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for free ice melt and more pre-treatment. Ice causes the worst falls, and treating early is cheaper than chipping later.

**One lead**

- *Today:* Enforcement is split among DPW, Inspectional Services and Traffic and Parking (Sec. 1-11(b)), with no lead named.
- *Proposed:* "The Mayor shall name one official to lead sidewalk snow clearing across departments: the winter plan, education, enforcement data and the yearly report."
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for one team to lead. Residents, the Council and PTAC would have one person to call.

**Signal push buttons and accessible parking**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall keep pedestrian push buttons at signals reachable from the cleared curb ramp, and clear accessible parking spaces on its streets and in its lots, including snow emergency lots, within 24 hours after the event ends."
- *Why:* These are the City's own accessible features, and federal rules require it to keep them working, allowing only brief interruptions (28 CFR 35.133).

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** a published plan and schedule for City sidewalks, bus stops and ramps, one person to call, and free sand or ice melt.
- **City staff:** binding deadlines and more crew hours, with liability questions for the Law Department; planning and logging time; buying and spreading ice melt. One lead coordinates DPW, ISD and Mobility. Crews also keep signal push buttons reachable and accessible parking spaces clear.

### Sec. 12-9: snow into streets, bike lanes and bus stops

Keep the ban, name contractors, add bike lanes, bus stops, crosswalks and ramps, and replace a temperature test no one can check in advance.

**Who**

- *Today:* "No owner, tenant or occupant of land or a building, or any agent thereof"
- *Proposed:* "No person, including a contractor clearing a driveway, parking lot or sidewalk"
- *Why:* Contractors clear many lots and driveways; naming them ends any debate over who is an "agent".

**Where**

- *Today:* "in any public way"
- *Proposed:* "onto a street, bike lane, bus stop, crosswalk or curb ramp"
- *Why:* Snow pushed into a bike lane or bus stop stays there long after the street is plowed, because no one is assigned to clear it.

**Exception**

- *Today:* "unless said snow or ice is broken up and spread evenly, to a thickness of no more than three inches, during daylight hours, when the mean temperature for that day is above 40 degrees Fahrenheit"
- *Proposed:* "On a street only (never a bike lane, bus stop, crosswalk or ramp): snow broken up and spread no more than 3 inches thick, in daylight, when the air temperature at that time is above 40°F."
- *Why:* A day's mean temperature is known only after the day ends, so no one can follow or enforce the current test.

**Fines**

- *Today:* "(b) ... fined in accordance with section 1-11"
- *Proposed:* Keep, and add: "The ticket goes to the owner of the property the snow came from."
- *Why:* One name to find, as in Sec. 12-8. The owner recovers from a careless contractor by contract.

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** pay the ticket if their contractor pushes snow into the street, a bike lane or a bus stop.
- **City staff:** a temperature test they can check on the spot.

### Sec. 12-2: water and ice from buildings

Water from a building freezes on the sidewalk on sunny days, not only during storms. Name the sources, require the fix, and give the rule a ticket.

**What's banned**

- *Today:* (a) "No person shall suffer his or her building or awning or other projection thereon to shed water on any sidewalk or street to the injury or inconvenience of travelers."
- *Proposed:* "No building, awning or other projection may shed or discharge water onto a sidewalk, curb ramp or bus stop. This includes roofs, gutters and downspouts, and discharge from sump pumps, heat pumps, furnaces, boilers and air conditioners. Water shall be piped under the sidewalk to the street or drain, or onto the owner's own ground."
- *Why:* "To the injury or inconvenience of travelers" invites argument after someone has already fallen. Naming the sources tells owners what to check. Auburn's bylaw (2017 text) names sump pumps and drains.

**Other discharge**

- *Today:* (b) bans water from washing cars "or for other purposes" flowing "upon or across the surface of any sidewalk".
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Already covers hoses and washing.

**Ice from a building**

- *Today:* None. Sec. 12-8 applies only after a weather event.
- *Proposed:* "Ice formed by water from a building is the owner's to treat and remove under Sec. 12-8, whenever it forms."
- *Why:* Downspout ice forms on sunny days after the storm, when no deadline is running.

**Falling ice and snow**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The owner shall promptly remove snow and ice that could fall onto the sidewalk from a roof, awning or sign, and mark the danger until it is removed."
- *Why:* Icicles and roof slides hit people on the sidewalk below. State law lets cities require this (M.G.L. c. 40 §21(2)); Chelsea's ordinance (2012 text) gives owners 24 hours.

**Fines**

- *Today:* Sec. 12-2 has no row in the Sec. 1-11(b) fine schedule.
- *Proposed:* "Add Sec. 12-2 to Sec. 1-11(b), with the same fines and enforcing personnel as Sec. 12-8."
- *Why:* Without a row in the fine schedule, inspectors have no ticket to write for a downspout icing the sidewalk.

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** a one-time fix for downspouts or sump pumps that drain onto the sidewalk; larger buildings watch for roof ice.
- **City staff:** a new ticket type.

### Sec. 12-18: space savers and objects in the path

The City treats space savers as illegal under a section titled "at night" that never names them. Say it outright, and keep bins and other objects out of the cleared path.

**Title**

- *Today:* "Obstacles in streets or sidewalks at night" (fine schedule: "overnight")
- *Proposed:* "Obstacles in streets and sidewalks"
- *Why:* The text already says "at any time"; the title invites the argument that daytime is fine.

**Space savers**

- *Today:* Not named. (a) bans "any vehicle or any other obstacle or article on any sidewalk or in any public or private way ... without first obtaining a permit".
- *Proposed:* "(e) No person may place a chair, cone or any other object in a street to hold a parking space. The City may remove and discard it at once. Each object is a separate offense."
- *Why:* The City's snow page already says it prohibits space savers and may collect and dispose of them; residents should find that in the code. Boston, by contrast, allows them for 48 hours after a snow emergency, except in the South End and Bay Village.

**Objects in the path**

- *Today:* Covered only in general by (a).
- *Proposed:* "No object, including a trash or recycling bin, may stand in a clear path, curb ramp or bus stop required by Sec. 12-8. On collection day, put bins at the curb edge in front of the snowbank."
- *Why:* Bins left in the cleared strip push wheelchairs into the street. Boston and Brookline give the same advice on bins.

**Fine**

- *Today:* $100 per offense; "each day the violation exists shall constitute a separate and new violation"
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** no saved parking spots; bins at the curb edge, out of the path.
- **City staff:** no change; the City already removes space savers.

### Sec. 1-11: fines, inspections and follow-up

Make the ticket automatic and the amount simple: inspectors must ticket every violation they find, two fine levels read from City records, and a flat clearing fee on top when City crews do the work. State law caps a ticket at $300, so pressure comes from a new ticket each day and the clearing fee, not bigger fines.

**Fine amounts**

- *Today:* 12-8 and 12-9: 1–3 units $100 / $200 / $300; 4+ units and businesses $150 / $300. Counted per property, Oct 1 to May 1.
- *Proposed:* "Each ticket under Sec. 12-2, 12-8 and 12-9 is $150 for an owner-occupied home (one with the residential tax exemption) and $300 for any other property: rentals, businesses, vacant property and construction sites. Untreated ice is $300 for any property. If the City clears under Sec. 12-8(c), the owner also pays the clearing fee."
- *Why:* Two amounts, both read from City records in seconds, and no offense history to look up (today's 1st/2nd/3rd count runs per property from Oct 1 to May 1). PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for higher fines for ice, landlords and developers; $300 is the state cap. Repeat failures still add up: a new ticket each day, plus the clearing fee.

**Construction sites**

- *Today:* Same fines as any property of its type.
- *Proposed:* "While a building, demolition or sidewalk-opening permit is open on the property, each ticket is $300, the state cap."
- *Why:* Sites have crews and equipment, so a missed deadline is a choice. Even an owner-occupied home pays $300 while a permit is open, and the permit is easy to check: it is open or it isn't. The bigger lever is the permit itself (see construction sites under Sec. 12-8).

**Who gets the ticket**

- *Today:* Not stated; any "person ... in charge" can be fined.
- *Proposed:* "A ticket names the owner of record, from the City's assessing records, and is mailed to the owner's tax-bill address. The inspector need not find out who lives in, rents or manages the property."
- *Why:* One name, found in seconds: no investigation, no appeals over the wrong person, and absentee owners learn of the problem.

**Duty to ticket**

- *Today:* Not in the code (in practice, "the citations range from a warning to up to $300", CBS Boston, Feb 19, 2025).
- *Proposed:* "An inspector who finds a violation shall issue a ticket. No warnings. The only exception is a property registered in the snow assistance program under Sec. 12-8(e)."
- *Why:* Takes the decision off inspectors and the administration: when neighbors push back, the answer is the ordinance, not someone's discretion. Exceptions are settled in advance, by registration, not on the sidewalk.

**Where inspectors look**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "Within 24 hours after the deadline for every event that leaves snow or ice on the ground, inspectors shall check bus routes, squares and school walking routes, whether or not anyone complained."
- *Why:* Complaints follow who reports: in 2025/26, Ward 1 filed 1,010 sidewalk reports and Ward 7 filed 142. Blocks where no one calls get no inspection today. Every storm counts, small ones included: residents tell PTAC that small storms leave the worst ice.

**Repeat tickets**

- *Today:* (a) "Each day a violation continues shall constitute a separate offense."
- *Proposed:* Keep, tied to the re-inspection in Sec. 12-8(c).
- *Why:* Makes the daily offense real. Cambridge ($50) and Worcester ($75) fine by the day.

**Language**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "Tickets and notices include how to pay, appeal and get help, in the languages the City publishes its snow rules in."
- *Why:* The City already publishes the rules in 9 languages; the ticket should match.

**Where the money goes**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "Fines and clearing charges under Sec. 12-8 shall fund the snow assistance program and City clearing of ramps and bus stops, subject to appropriation."
- *Why:* Ties enforcement to help. Fines normally go to the City's general fund, so this needs a dedicated fund set up by the Council and Mayor; the Law Department should advise on the form.

**Unpaid fines**

- *Today:* (c) may block local licenses and permits, or become a municipal charges lien.
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** $150 instead of $100 for a first ticket, and no warning on a first miss.
- **Owners of rentals, business properties and construction sites:** $300 instead of $100 or $150 for a first ticket.
- **City staff:** no judgment calls on the sidewalk and no offense history to track, but more tickets to process and more inspector hours, since every storm counts, small ones included.

### New Sec. 12-8B: notices, reporting and open data

Tell people the deadline every time it snows, and publish what enforcement and City crews actually did, so the Council can judge whether the rules work.

**Deadline notice**

- *Today:* None required by the code. The City posts snow emergency notices and occasional sidewalk reminders.
- *Proposed:* "For every winter weather event, the City shall announce, by City Alert and on its website, the time the event ended and the clearing deadline, in the languages it publishes its snow rules in."
- *Why:* Residents tell PTAC that small storms leave the worst ice, and the code requires no notice for any storm. The City has done this before: a March 5, 2023 reminder (no emergency) gave the deadline as 1 p.m., and the Feb 2026 emergency notices stated one too. Do it every time.

**Season reminder**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Each November, the City shall send every household and business the sidewalk rules, with the water or tax bill or by mail, including photos of acceptable and unacceptable clearing; the duties of owners and permit holders; suitable tools and de-icers; the snow assistance and teen shoveler programs; and how to hire and oversee a snow contractor."
- *Why:* Residents tell PTAC the first December storm is when people relearn the rules; a November mailing reaches them first, including everyone who moved in since last winter. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for this content, noting that the City's snow materials focus on cars.

**How a 311 report ends**

- *Today:* Every sidewalk report reads "Closed".
- *Proposed:* "A 311 sidewalk request shall close with its outcome: clear on inspection, ticket, cleared by the City, or not found."
- *Why:* Today "Closed" can mean anything; 754 cases were closed on one day, Feb 6, 2026, 663 of them over a month old.

**Open data**

- *Today:* 311 requests only.
- *Proposed:* "The City shall publish, weekly from November to April: inspections and tickets under Sec. 12-2, 12-8, 12-9 and 12-18 (date, time, street and ward, without house numbers); City clearing and liens under Sec. 12-8(c); the clearing log of Sec. 12-8A; and each snow emergency with start and end times."
- *Why:* None of this is public today. Patterns (blocks that fail every storm, wards no one inspects) stay invisible.

**Yearly report**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "By June 30, the Commissioner of Public Works and the Director of Inspectional Services shall report to the City Council: complaints, inspections, tickets, City clearings and liens, and time to clear each layer under Sec. 12-8A, by ward and by event, next to each event's snowfall."
- *Why:* Lets the Council see whether these changes work, and adjust. Brookline's bylaw (2012 text) required a yearly report on education, enforcement and help. It can join the yearly pedestrian report the Council already receives under Sec. 12-116.6, which tracks the City's ADA Transition Plan goals.

**Blocked accessible routes**

- *Today:* None. Every 311 sidewalk report goes into the same queue.
- *Proposed:* "311 shall offer a request type for a blocked curb ramp, crosswalk opening, bus stop or path that keeps a person with a disability from leaving home or reaching transit. These requests go to the City's ADA Coordinator and shall be answered within 12 hours."
- *Why:* A blocked ramp can keep a wheelchair user at home for days. The City must already name an ADA Coordinator and keep a way to resolve complaints (28 CFR 35.107); this gives winter complaints a fast lane.

**Accessible formats**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Notices, the season guide and open data under this section shall be provided in formats accessible to people with disabilities."
- *Why:* Federal rules require the City's communications to work for people with disabilities (28 CFR 35.160), and its websites and apps to meet WCAG 2.1 AA from April 26, 2027 (28 CFR 35.200).

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** told the deadline after every storm, and a guide each November.
- **City staff:** the notices, a mailing, 311 close codes, weekly data and a yearly report, all in accessible formats; the ADA Coordinator answers urgent blocked-route requests within 12 hours.

## Attachment B: Beyond the ordinance

A few limits sit in state law or with other agencies. The Council can't change them alone, but it can ask.

| Topic | What limits Somerville today | What to ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine cap | A ticket can't exceed $300 ([M.G.L. c. 40 §21D](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40/Section21D)), and an ordinance penalty can't exceed $300 per offense ([c. 40 §21](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40/Section21)). | A home rule petition, as Boston obtained in [Acts of 2016, c. 352](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/SessionLaws/Acts/2016/Chapter352): higher fines for commercial properties, large landlords and construction sites that fail repeatedly, not for small homes. Boston's act allows up to $1,500. |
| Legal basis | Sec. 12-8 cites both c. 40 §21 and [c. 85 §5](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter85/Section5). c. 85 §5 caps a city's penalty at $50; c. 40 §21(3) aims the penalty at "the owner of abutting property or his agent". | The Law Department confirms which statute supports each proposal, that ticketing only the owner of record fits c. 40 §21(3), that a flat clearing fee counts as a charge for the City's work, outside the $300 ticket cap, and that Inspectional Services may hold a project's inspections over unpaid sidewalk tickets. |
| MBTA bus stops and stations | The MBTA clears stops on its listed frequent routes within 24 hours; in Somerville, routes 77, 104 and 109; who clears Green Line station entrances is not published. | A written agreement listing every Somerville stop and station entrance, who clears it and by when, including while buses run during a storm (Sec. 12-8A). Monthly counts from the MBTA's bus cameras of vehicles blocking stops on Somerville routes, once tickets start on Jan 1, 2027. |
| State roads | The City lists State property last in its clearing order, with no contact. | Agreements with MassDOT and DCR for the sidewalks along McGrath Highway, Alewife Brook Parkway and other state roads, with the same deadlines. |
| Business districts | Each small business hires its own contractor, if any. | City help for business associations to hire shared snow contractors for whole districts (PTAC letter, item 8). |
| Winter-ready sidewalks | Not part of this ordinance. | Winter in the City's sidewalk design standards: drainage at ramps, grading and materials that shed water, and a look at heated sidewalks in the busiest squares (PTAC letter, item 11). |
| City clearing of every sidewalk | Owners clear their own frontage; a few US cities clear all sidewalks themselves. | A Council order for a cost and feasibility study of City clearing of every sidewalk, with funding options, as Minneapolis has done (PTAC letter, item 13). |
