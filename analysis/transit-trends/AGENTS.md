# Transit over time (`analysis/transit-trends/`)

Agent guide. Start with the root [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md). What the page shows, how it's counted, its limits and sources are in [`README.md`](README.md); keep it current when the page changes.

`docs/transit-trends.html` (somerwalks.org/transit-trends.html, a tile on the home page): weekday boardings at Somerville's bus stops and the T stations its people use, every fall since 2016. Analysis from the MBTA's counts, labeled as such.

## Build

`npm run trends` (Python standard library). It writes `output/trends.html` from `trends_template.html` (the data replaces `/*DATA*/null`) and copies it to `docs/transit-trends.html`: never edit the copy. Cached in `../../.cache/`: `mbta-bus-ridership.zip` (shared with `analysis/bus-trips`), `gtfs-stops-<feed date>.json` (each fall's stop locations, read from the archived feed with HTTP range requests, no full download), `trends-towns.geojson`, `trends-rail-<season>.json`, the Bluebikes monthly zips (about 400 MB for Sep–Nov 2016–2025) and `trends-bikes-<year>.json`. Also `trends-acs-<year>.json` and `trends-lodes-<year>.json` (a year not published yet isn't cached, so the next build asks again; a year the Bureau never published for Somerville is cached as empty). Delete a cache file to refresh it. The build prints each season's totals; check them against the previous run.

**A new season** (the MBTA adds a fall each year): the bus file picks it up by itself. For the T, add the new rail file's ArcGIS item to `RAIL_ITEMS` (kind `csv` for a downloadable CSV, `service` for a feature service with `parent_station`, `old` for the 2017–19 service keyed by `stop_name`).

## Choices to keep

- **Which stations:** the seven inside Somerville plus Porter and Sullivan Square (`STATIONS`). Lechmere is left out on purpose (close to the line, about 220 m, but next to industrial land rather than homes, few in Somerville use it). Change the list only with a reason on the page.
- **Inside Somerville** is tested against the full-detail outline (`somerville_exact()`); `towns()` is simplified (about 20 m) and only draws the map. With the simplified one a Cambridge Bluebikes dock and a bus stop on Elm St flipped sides.
- **Somerville stops** are matched to the city outline per season, with that season's stop locations, never today's list (stops and routes change: the 86 left Somerville in Dec 2024, the 109 came in).
- **A stop id missing from that fall's feed** (the last feed of the fall can miss stops detoured or closed earlier in it: 14 in 2019) takes its location from the nearest other fall's feed. `start_hour()` reads `7:05:00` and the fractions of a day some Fall 2025 rows use.
- **Weekday** is Monday–Friday: from Fall 2025 the file splits `Wkdy` (Mon–Thu) and `Fri`; `bus()` combines them 4 to 1.
- **Routes** are named as on the other bus pages (`747` → CT2, `194` → 89/93); routes under `MIN_ROUTE` (50) riders in every season are dropped.
- **Bluebikes** (`bluebikes()`): trips by start station, each station placed at its dock (a classic-bike trip's start position; from 2024 e-bike trips carry GPS positions), Sep–Nov, federal holidays out (`holidays()`, Veterans Day as observed). Docks are counted as places (`places()`: ids within 40 m are one dock, so renumbered ids count once; "Temp Winter Station" ids left out). The file names change over time (`hubway`, `bluebikes`, `.csv.zip`): they're found from the bucket listing.
- **Who's working** (`acs()`, `lodes()`): the survey's 1-year tables for Somerville city: B08006 for the way to work, not B08301, which is blank for Somerville in 2022 and 2023 (the table service lists the city with empty values; `acs()` treats an empty `_001E` as missing). There are none for 2020 (the service answers 204 or 400): shown as "no survey". LODES is summed over Somerville's census blocks from the LODES crosswalk (`trends-lodes-xwalk.csv.gz`), not a tract range, which takes in a Medford block. The page compares survey years with their margins (`beyondMargin`: a gap bigger than √(m0² + m1²)) and says "about as many" when it isn't; "residents with a job" is B23025 employed, "residents who worked" is the commute table's total (the denominator of the shares). The jobs chart's note explains the 2018 jump (about 5,800 "management of companies" jobs first counted at one block in tract 350105). Keep the note about hybrid work next to the transit-commuter line: the survey's "usual mode" can't show fewer days a week.
- **Fall 2025 rail CSV:** a station's rows (per route, direction, hour) each average over their own service days, and Green Line branches serve the Medford stations on different numbers of days: add `total_ons` and divide by the station's most service days, don't add averages.
- **Fare-gate entries** (`gates()`, the MBTA's Gated Station Entries, item `7859894a…`, `GSE_by_year.zip`, about 75 MB, cached as `mbta-gated-entries.zip`): average weekday entries, Sep–Nov, holidays out, days with no entries at a station (closures, shutdowns) left out, for Davis, Porter, Assembly and Sullivan (`GATED`; the Green Line Extension has no gates). Each station carries them as `g` next to its boardings `v`. The page uses them two ways and never mixes them into a boardings number: hatched bars (the line's color, lighter) for the falls with no published boardings (2016, 2020–22), and a dashed "T fare gates" line in the 2019 comparison, the same measure every fall. Gate entries tracked boardings within 0–5% in 2017–19 and drifted after (Assembly 2025: 4,891 boardings, 3,122 gate entries); the stations note says so.
- **Missing T years** (2016, 2020–22) are a short dashed, hatched cap above the bus column labeled "T not counted": missing, never zero, and never a height that looks like a value.
- **Map stops** are merged across seasons when they sit within `SAME_STOP` (12 m) of each other (a renumbered or moved stop is one place, the busiest first); farther apart, a stop shows as gone in one fall and new in the other. Tried 25 and 40 m: the gone stops stay, so they're real route changes. The map note counts them, and names the 86 only when the falls picked span the Dec 2024 redesign.
- **Page order:** the answer first (a computed headline sentence, then four tiles under one "A weekday in Fall …, against Fall 2019" line), then the decade, back to 2019, why (who's working), the stations, the map, the routes, the hours, Bluebikes; "All the numbers" and "How it's counted" fold away at the end. Keep notes to one line.
- **How full are the buses?** (`profiles()`, `route_paths()`, `trip_loads()`): `trip_loads()` reads the ridership file again for Fall 2019 and the latest fall, only the profile routes, and keeps each weekday departure's load at each stop (and its sample days; under 3 are dropped). `profiles()` builds, per direction, the latest fall's stop list along the path (`s`), averages per period (`avg.b|n.r|d`: rush per `rush()`, the MBTA's 7:00–8:59 and 16:00–18:29, and all day) and the latest fall's departures (`dep`: start minute, first stop index, loads with -1 where a stop is skipped). That is most of the page's weight (about 160 KB of 280 KB). Routes are those with at least `MIN_ROUTE` riders in the latest fall; and places stops along the latest feed's path (cached `trends-paths-<feed date>.json`, read from `trips.txt` and `shapes.txt` by range requests). Keep its filters (150 m off the path, past its ends, served by under 30% of rush trips): without the last one, school trips on the 95 and 101 doubled their peaks. The page shows one route at a time (chips), stop names in the tooltip (`prof.names`, indexed to keep the payload small).
- **Every route:** cards with a small chart on wide screens; under 600 px a ranked list (route, 2019 → latest, change). Each route carries riders `v` and trips `t` (weekday trips that stop in Somerville, counted in `bus()` from variant, direction and start time; `Wkdy` only from 2025). The card draws both as a share of 2019 on one axis (no second axis); a route with no 2019 riders (the 109) shows riders only. Trips are gray, like other reference lines.

## Charts and colors

Colors were checked with the dataviz palette validator on the navy surface (`#10243A`): the stacked columns go bus `#4F98DE`, Red `#DA291C`, Green `#1B9B6A`, Orange `#D57200` (red and orange never touch); the index chart uses bus blue, the T `#C08A14`, Bluebikes `#2FA37A` and every MBTA bus as a gray reference `#8A9BB0`; "Who's working" uses worked from home `#C8609C`, transit amber, drove alone gray, walked cyan `#45B7C9` (used nowhere else) and biked the Bluebikes green (bikes keep one color), then people with jobs in an ice ramp that no other chart uses (residents working `#DCE6F2`, residents' jobs `#A9C4E4`, jobs in Somerville `#7690B0`); the map is diverging orange `#E36F28` (fewer riders) to purple `#9575E3` (more) through gray, new stops a purple ring, gone stops an orange ring, T stations white squares, so on the map orange only ever means fewer and bus blue isn't reused for "more". The route list and cards use the same meaning in text tints (`#F2A374` fewer, `#B9A3F0` more). Every chart has a legend, selective direct labels, a hover tooltip, an `aria-label`, and its numbers in the table. Axis scales (`ticks()`) always reach past the largest value. Map station labels are placed biggest first where they overlap nothing (short names on phones).
